# Content Catalog — Design Candidates (not production promises)

## Gameplay groups

| Group | Intended role | Constraints |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New normal monsters | Fresh ingredients and interesting natural encounters | Baseline drops always available by ordinary kill |
| Selected special monsters | Visibly distinct combat behavior and optional bonus drop | Weakness optional, never required for core recipes |
| Rare encounters / bosses | Higher-risk special materials and memorable presentation | Difficulty from readable behavior, not unexplained kill conditions |
| Vanilla mob ingredient extensions | Renewed reasons to adventure in vanilla regions | Avoid damaging vanilla loot and balance |
| Crop/herb/spice | Farming tie-in and recipe variety | No tedious processing chain |
| Kitchen stations | Cutting, heating, stewing as genuinely distinct actions | Keep station count low until playtested |
| Food | Hunger/saturation, short helpful bonuses, sale value | No dozens of hidden buffs |
| Restaurant tools/furniture | Place, register, open/close and decorate a real restaurant | Free build choices |
| Customers/staff | Service and workload assistance | Few jobs with clear responsibility |
| Unified guide | Monster, dish, ingredient, restaurant information | Discover naturally; no grind tasks |

## Planned early slice (not final full-release counts)

- Two normal monsters with clearly visible ordinary drops, one with **optional** readable timing bonus.
- Four ingredients, 4–6 dishes, 1–2 cooking stations, one restaurant counter, one customer type and one assisting employee.
- At least one recipe usable outside restaurant, one satisfying sale and one meaningful purchase with earnings.
- One integrated guide page flows from encounter → ingredient → recipe without long texts.
- First playable test must show the entire hunt → cook → serve → earn → reinvest loop in a normal Bedrock survival world.

## Scope gates

No fixed promises of '60 dishes' or '25 mobs' until representative gameplay meets real-world fun/UX/performance gates. Expand quality and interactions before quantity.
